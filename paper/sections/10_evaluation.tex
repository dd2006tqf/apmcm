The table separates two kinds of uncertainty. The area of new inundation in Hengzhou is uncertain by about a factor of two, almost entirely because of the terrain and probability thresholds, which cannot be calibrated without local labels. The decisions taken from it are much more stable: the city grade never leaves ``general'', the unit ranking keeps a Spearman correlation of at least 0.889 under every alternative input of 16 July, and the relief plan changes only when the flood extent on the roads changes. The largest single change in the ranking (Spearman 0.662) comes from using the 4 July instead of the 16 July image, i.e. from new information, not from a modeling choice.

\subsection{Strengths}

Validation is spatially blocked with a buffer, every tuning step uses a separate calibration fold, and the leave-one-cluster-out test leaves IoU 0.508, so the numbers reported here estimate new areas rather than the training set. The objective is also the quantity a disaster report quotes: a 50 px minimum mapping unit takes LightGBM from $+9.2\%$ to $-0.2\%$ (IoU 0.598 to 0.618), area calibration brings the transferable M6 to $+1.45\%$, and grade accuracy moves from 54.3\% to 86.2\%. Uncertainty survives into the answers: conformal tile intervals cover 87.8\% of the test tiles, every grade carries $\pi_{jg}$, and Hengzhou is reported as a scenario grid. The chain also closes numerically: one raster $F$ defines the hazard of Problem~4 and the cut network of Problem~5, the Problem~4 population and index set demand and priority, and the solver returns status optimal with zero gap on every run.

\subsection{Boundaries of the Present Evidence}

The Hengzhou estimate rests on the physics guards rather than on a spectral check: with no labeled flood sample and no usable optical scene, drainage, terrain and multi-date change carry the transfer, and they remove 82.6\% of the transferred mask, almost all of it permanent water that $W_0$ identifies. The 27 June baseline was itself a wet-season state, so the net change is negative and partly seasonal, and the analysis reports the seasonal part separately from the flood part. A co-registration edge adds about 3.4\,km$^2$ of spurious pre-flood water and recession, well inside the width of the reported interval. The relief model works at the resolution of town centers, with 2020 population and an aggregate fleet budget rather than vehicle routes; at that resolution isolation, not supply, limits relief, and that finding holds under every assumption tested.

\subsection{Innovations}

Two modeling choices were settled before training. First, the labels were read rather than assumed: on all 65.5\,M pixels they mark new inundation, so the recognizer learns $F$ directly while Problem~3 still enforces $F\cap W_0=\emptyset$ with an independent $W_0$, which removes a systematic over-count of permanent water and keeps the training target identical to the problem's definition. Second, the area is the calibration target, not the 0.5 or maximal F1 threshold that such models usually use: probabilities are calibrated isotonically and the threshold is chosen so that the mask area equals the calibrated expected area \eqref{eq:tauA}, with conformal intervals per tile. Total area error of M6 falls from $+4.1\%$ to $+1.45\%$ without touching the network, maps and reported areas stay identical, and the uncalibrated M2 reaches $+0.9\%$ after the 50 px MMU but has no conformal interval.



Two further choices concern how a result is read. Grades are separated where adjacent components of a Gaussian mixture of the logit flood share are equally likely \eqref{eq:gmm_bayes}, the thresholds carry bootstrap intervals, and each unit receives a probability vector \eqref{eq:grade_mc}, so the grading is reproducible from data and unreliable grades advertise themselves. The flood map then enters the relief model twice: the same raster removes roads and depots and generates demand and priority, so the worth of the next satellite pass becomes the change of the optimal unmet share (3.9 points), a number an emergency manager can weigh against the cost of extra acquisitions.



\subsection{Conclusions}

We built one chain from images to relief. On the official data, a LightGBM pixel classifier reaches IoU 0.618 with a total area error of $-0.2\%$ after a 50 px MMU; a dual-branch gated SAR--optical U-Net reaches IoU 0.797 and $+0.9\%$ in-domain after the same MMU, while its calibrated modality-dropout variant used for transfer reaches IoU 0.730 and $+1.45\%$. Four intensity grades are separated at flood shares of 5.9\%, 53.0\% and 96.9\%. Transferred to Hengzhou with co-registered Sentinel-1 data and physical guards, the model finds 26.90\,km$^2$ of new inundation on 16 July (0.78\% of the city, grade ``general'') and a net change of $-52.53$\,km$^2$ caused by water receding after a wet baseline. On 393 settlement catchments the assessment ranks Shitang, Maling and Luancheng as the most affected zones, with a ranking that is stable under weight perturbation. The relief MILP opens six depots and serves 7316 of 13,948 people in need; the remaining need is cut off by water, not short of supplies, which defines where the authority should send boats rather than trucks. That share equals the weighted demand of the isolated units, so it is a floor and not slack; 600 scenarios that perturb both the demand and the roads put its CVaR$_{95}$ at 0.651 and never make the plan infeasible\cite{deepseek2026,qwen2026,doubao2026}.
